# Six Player Generator

Create a decision-ready product narrative in connected prose, adapted from the user's 6-pager Generator workflow. Keep the requested product, audience, geography, date and business context. Despite the skill's display name, its output is a six-pager, not a six-player game.

## Establish the decision and evidence

Lead with the recommendation, customer benefit and concrete decision requested. Establish whether the task concerns a new product, an improvement or a counterfactual rebuild. For a rebuild, state which company assets remain available and which must be created; do not quietly reuse the existing product's hardware, software, approvals or installed base.

Use available context before asking questions. Make reasonable, labeled assumptions about nonblocking gaps. Ask when an unresolved choice would materially change the strategy. Do not invent interviews, customer quotes, research findings, engineering commitments or approved budgets.

For current market, technical or regulatory claims, verify authoritative primary sources when browsing is available. Record source dates, access date, region and direct links. Distinguish a manufacturer's claim from independent evidence and an announced feature from one already available. If verification is unavailable, label the item unverified or an assumption rather than describing it as current fact.

Mark facts, assumptions, hypotheses, targets and recommendations clearly. Attach citations near factual claims and include a compact source list. Do not let an illustrative number become an asserted market statistic elsewhere in the narrative.

## Build the six-page narrative

Use this default sequence; adapt headings and page allocation to the user's decision. Write a narrative rather than six slides or an unfilled questionnaire.

1. **Customer problem and strategic choice.** Include a 20–30 second elevator pitch, specific pain, why it matters, current workarounds, primary and secondary segments, exclusions, vision and why this organization can win.
2. **Discovery and differentiated value.** Explain available evidence, unresolved hypotheses, alternatives including doing nothing, competition, differentiated value, reach and proposed customer research. Give a low-cost proof of concept and explicit conditions for proceeding or stopping.
3. **MVP experience and acceptance.** Describe the first-use and ordinary-day experience, features, non-goals and testable customer outcomes. Address APIs, AI/ML suitability, accessibility and necessary platform capabilities. Treat compliance and engineering commitments as pending unless evidenced.
4. **Technical and privacy feasibility.** Explain architecture, build-versus-buy choices, dependencies, validation, power or performance constraints, data collection, processing, consent, sharing, retention, deletion, security and relevant regulatory boundaries. Scale detail to the product's actual risks.
5. **Launch and business case.** Cover positioning, launch sequence, channels, pricing, support, business model, unit economics, staffing, fixed investment, variable cost, opportunity cost and sensitivities. Where useful, show top-down TAM/SAM/SOM and a bottom-up demand model with explicit time periods and denominators. Include a brief proposed launch press-release paragraph, labeled hypothetical, for a major customer-facing launch.
6. **Success and decisions.** Define a prioritized north-star metric, customer outcomes, leading indicators and guardrails with denominators, time windows and proposed targets. Include risks and mitigations, a phased roadmap with exit criteria and owners by role, public and internal launch FAQs, and the precise approval, experiment or investment decision requested.

For an incremental feature, scale business-case and launch material proportionally. Do not copy company-specific Cisco or ThousandEyes fields into unrelated products. Include upgrade or migration plans only when an installed product exists. If engineering planning status matters, distinguish committed, still planning and not committed without inventing an outcome.

## Review and deliver

Check that the proposed differentiation survives the competitive analysis; features, economics, timing and metrics agree; and launch depends on evidence rather than dates alone. Recalculate financial arithmetic and distinguish retail sales, net revenue, gross margin, contribution and cash needs. Give market models and forecasts their own assumptions and avoid implied causal health or business claims without evidence.

Deliver an editable document when requested and supported. Use the available document authoring workflow, render it and inspect all pages. A six-page document means six rendered pages; a Markdown file with six sections does not establish pagination. Keep readable typography and revise content instead of shrinking it to illegibility. State any unresolved delivery limitation plainly. If only chat or Markdown is available, provide six clearly labeled sections without claiming verified page count.

Use [the Apple Watch example](apple-watch-six-pager-october-2026.md) when a worked example helps establish narrative density and coverage. It is a dated counterfactual strategy example; its facts, assumptions, budgets, pricing, timelines and market choices must not be reused as defaults for another product or treated as freshly verified research.
